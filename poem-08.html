ENTRY 08 · FREE TO READ

MOVE ON?

They say there are other tables,
other menus,
other places to dine.

Visit a new restaurant,
after building a huge mansion
and a little outhouse in its garden?

The heart aches for something
that no longer belongs to it.
The hand trembles
at the sight of what remains.

There was a restaurant
I had waited so long to open.

I stood outside its doors
every day, every night,
even before it officially opened.

I waited for the lights to turn on,
for the doors to finally unfold,
for the first meal
I had dreamt of tasting.

And then...

I left that restaurant long ago.
But how could I not check on the chef?
The finest one I'd ever known.

The table where I once sat
now belongs to another customer.

I stopped visiting that restaurant
because the chef had started charging too much.

My heart grew weak
from all the meals it had been served.

Too sweet sometimes,
until my teeth began to ache.

Too bland sometimes,
leaving me hungry even after eating.

Too spicy sometimes,
making me drink more tears
than water.

And even that wasn't enough.

What more could I have done?
What else was I supposed to give?

I still don't understand.

Perhaps I miss the restaurant.
Perhaps I miss the food.
Or perhaps,
I only miss the chef.

I still have the receipt
from the very first meal.

I was punctual with every visit.
I arrived at my allotted time,
sometimes even earlier,
before the restaurant opened in the morning.

I never wanted to miss a serving.

Then, one day,
I received a call.

The new customer
wasn't being charged at all.

Nothing.

Free food.

Free.

Me?

Huh?

The customer arrived late, too.

And yet,
the food was served without a price.

I stared at the receipt in my hands.

I remembered every meal,
every price,
every time I had waited outside those doors.

And I wondered—

Was it always this easy
for someone else to be fed?

And even if someday
the chef decides to charge less,
I'd still give more
than the exact amount.

Because I've always felt
that way about the chef.

Somewhere in my heart,
I still believe
the chef will keep a place for me
in that restaurant,
and prepare my favourite meal.

It's okay.

I don't like the food they serve there anymore.

I don't.

I just don't.

Please, God,
make me hate that food.

And hey, new customer—

Pay sometimes, okay?

The chef's a nice one.
