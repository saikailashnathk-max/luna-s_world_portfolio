EIGHTEEN

Called weird since childhood.
ADHD? Maybe.
OCD? Absolutely.

Life has always been hard for all,
and so is mine.

My silence.
My presence.
Neither mattered.

I only wanted to hear,
"You're doing great."
Nothing more.

At one point, life became overwhelming.

It veiled my heart with cloth adorned with thorns,
choked my throat with claws,
crumpled my body with needles.

As I bled, I smiled.

They laughed.

My pillows drank my tears almost every day.

"A hypocrite."
That's all they said.

I forgot how to smile.

I forgot what happiness felt like.

3 a.m. crash-outs became usual.
Panic attacks became usual.

Not a soul to comfort me
as I drowned in my own salty ocean.

Maybe, just like in the movies,
my eighteenth birthday would heal me.

So I thought.

The anticipated day came sooner than I expected.

Hopefully, I entered the forest at midnight,
only to find that the well was dry.

No water.

Just dry.

Then I heard an echo:

"You've changed."

I ran out of words.

I tried refilling the well with my tears,

but it never filled.

I tried,

again,

and again.

The echo never knew

my heart was already in pieces—

broken,

broken by the souls I live with.

My mouth was sealed with leeches.

It waited,

then left.

Just left.

I didn't beg.

I couldn't speak.

That day,

I lost myself—

my smile,

my hope,

my faith in goodness.

Everything.

Lost between pages and tears,

I learned
happiness is ephemeral.

"You're a burden."

The knife found
a throat already wounded.

No more tears.

I lay there,

not dead—

just the version of me
that still believed.

Dead.

Just dead.

What more could I say?
