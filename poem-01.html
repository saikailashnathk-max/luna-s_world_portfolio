DO YOU HATE THE RAIN, TOO?

Bizarre days in the middle of the third week of July.

A new place. New humans. New souls.

Initially, I was excited.

Then came the real saga.

My heartbeat slowed.

My vision became blurry.

Hopeless nights.

Suffocated, I called everyone. No one understood my language, for I spoke in blood and sweat that were barely visible.

I lost the whole point of living.

Just pain. Not physical.

Psychological, emotional and spiritual.

No one to listen to my drama.

I had to get things off my chest, because my heart was a whale.

The earth moved a little too slowly every. single. day.

My limbs grew numb.

Just me and my unspoken thoughts.

Unheard prayers.

There was a time when I had shut the window to a particular view.

A forest. Lush green. A calm, soothing scent. But treacherous.

For I knew that some windows are meant to remain shut, no matter how beautiful the view may be.

I had no other hopes.

The pain became unbearable, and I returned to open the very window I had once shut.

Maybe it would heal me.

I thought.

Suffocated, I tried calling everyone again. No one understood my language, for I spoke in blood and sweat that were barely visible.

I stared at the view a little too long when the butterfly showed up.

The sole soul that inhabited that forest.

We spoke. Somehow, it healed me again.

It did.

The heart that had wished to disappear all along finally found happiness and meaning.

It was fickle.

It didn't last long.

The butterfly wasn't a butterfly. It was a leech.

It sucked the blood out of me.

I cried silently, knowing my screams would scare it away.

The butterfly

found another human deep within the forest that looked so magical.

Left me and was gone — forever.

I kept looking for the butterfly.

Everywhere. Just everywhere.

It left me forever, leaving me in the rain.

It went to seek shelter deep within the forest.

I never closed the window after that.

Whenever I pass by it, I still see the butterfly, but with another human.

With other flowers.

I still cry thinking about the butterfly.

I loved the rain. Now I hate it.

Hey, butterfly, do you hate the rain, too?
